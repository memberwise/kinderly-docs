Parents don't check your app on a Tuesday morning to find out whether you're
open. They check the calendar on their phone. A **calendar link** puts your
closure days and center events into that calendar, and keeps them there.

Find them in **Manage → Settings → Calendar Links**.

## What a calendar link is

It's a subscription, not a download. Someone adds the link once, and from then
on their calendar refreshes itself — usually every few hours. Add a snow day
here on Sunday night and it appears on their phone without anyone doing
anything.

It works in Google Calendar, Outlook and Apple Calendar. There's nothing to
install and nobody has to sign in to Kinderly.

There are two kinds:

| Kind | What's in it | Who it's for |
|---|---|---|
| **Center events & closures** | Events and closure days you've posted | Families, and staff |
| **One person's shifts** | That person's shifts and approved time off | That one staff member |

A staff calendar carries only that person's own schedule. It never shows a
colleague's shifts, and time off shows only its type — PTO, Sick — never a
reason.

## Creating one

1. Choose the calendar and, for a staff schedule, the person.
2. Give it a name you'll recognise later — "Family calendar", "Ada's shifts".
3. Click **Create link**, then **Copy link**.

Send that link to whoever needs it. The list shows when each one was last used,
so you can tell at a glance which links people actually subscribed to.

## Adding it to a calendar

Tell people to use their calendar's **subscribe** option — not import, which
takes a one-off copy that never updates again.

- **Google Calendar** — Other calendars → **+** → From URL
- **Outlook** — Add calendar → **Subscribe from web**
- **Apple Calendar** — File → **New Calendar Subscription**

:::caution[Subscribe, don't import]
Importing gives someone a frozen snapshot. It will look right for a week and
then quietly stop matching your closure days, which is worse than not having it
at all.
:::

## The link is the password

Anyone holding the link can see that calendar. There's no sign-in step, because
calendar apps have no way to do one — they just fetch the address.

That's fine for what's in these feeds, and it's why there's nothing in them that
shouldn't travel: no child's name, no family's details, no pay information. But
treat the link itself the way you'd treat a password.

## Revoking

**Revoke** kills a link immediately. Use it when a link has gone somewhere you
didn't intend, or when a staff member leaves.

Two things worth knowing:

- Anything already sitting in someone's calendar stays there. Revoking stops it
  updating; it doesn't reach into their phone and remove past entries.
- You can't un-revoke. You'd create a new link and hand it out again — so
  revoking a family calendar means asking every family to re-subscribe.

Revoked links stay in the list, greyed out, so you can see what you've killed
rather than wondering whether you ever created it.
