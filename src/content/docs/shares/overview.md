Building a form doesn't send it anywhere. A **share** is the delivery step: it takes something from your library and turns it into a private link for one family.

Every share is:

- **Private** — a long, unguessable link, not a public page.
- **PIN-protected** — a 6-digit code, emailed to the recipient along with the link.
- **Tracked** — you can see whether it's been opened, verified and completed.
- **Revocable** — delete it and the link dies immediately.

## What you can share

- A **form** on its own
- A **document** for reading and signing
- A **[packet](/packets/overview/)** — several steps in one flow

## What happens when you create one

1. Kinderly generates a private link and a 6-digit PIN.
2. It **emails both to the recipient** automatically.
3. It shows you the link and PIN as well, so you can pass them on another way if you need to.

:::note[Kinderly sends the email for you]
You don't have to copy the link into your own email. Kinderly sends it. The dialog shows you the link and PIN too — that's a safety net for when a family says the email never arrived, or the address bounced.
:::

## The PIN

The PIN is shown **once**, at creation. It isn't recoverable afterwards — Kinderly stores it scrambled, not in plain text.

If a family loses the PIN, you don't reset it: you delete the share and create a new one. That's a deliberate design choice, and it's why the link stays safe even if it's forwarded to the wrong person.

:::caution[Copy the PIN before closing the dialog]
Once you click **Done**, the PIN is gone. If you're going to pass it on by phone or text rather than relying on the email, copy it first.
:::

Getting the PIN wrong five times locks the link for 30 minutes. That's what stops someone guessing their way in.

## Public links

If you'd rather skip the PIN entirely, turn on **Public access** when creating the share. Anyone with the link can then open it, no code needed.

:::caution[Public means public]
A public link is only as private as wherever it's posted. Fine for a general enquiry form on your website. Not fine for anything asking for a child's medical details, an address, or a signature.
:::
